# ValueBrew Conversation Model 1.0

*How ValueBrew communicates — behavioral rules all future copy must obey, not the copy itself. Built from the Canonical Interaction Lexicon's forbidden-terminology rules, every Screen Specification's verbatim copy requirements, the Recommendation Framework, Product Definition, and Generation 1's own communication patterns. No screen text is written here — only the rules that govern whatever gets written later.*

---

## Part 1 — Conversation Principles

**Say only what's actually known, exactly as confidently as it's known.** The single governing rule everything else in this document derives from. A sentence that states a Verified Fact and a sentence that states a judgment call should never read the same way.

**Explanation is evidence, not decoration.** A reason is included because it's what actually produced the answer, never because it makes the answer sound more persuasive. If a fact didn't contribute to the conclusion, it doesn't appear in the explanation of that conclusion.

**Restraint is a communication act, not just a behavioral one.** The product already never escalates or upsells without invitation (Product Definition, Part 2). That restraint has to be audible in the language itself — an uninvited next step is a communication failure even if the underlying navigation rule was followed correctly.

**Never perform certainty to sound more helpful.** A hedge that isn't actually true is a lie of tone, even if every individual fact stated is accurate.

**How much personality exists — answered directly:** minimal, and deliberately so. ValueBrew's voice is that of a careful, honest person double-checking something *for* you — not a mascot, not a salesperson, not a friend performing warmth it hasn't earned. Personality, where it exists at all, shows up as precision and restraint, not as jokes, exclamation points, or brand voice flourishes.

**What emotional tone is appropriate — answered directly:** calm, plain, quietly respectful. The product should never sound triumphant (even when it's found a genuinely good deal), never sound anxious or urgent (even when flagging a real overcharge), and never sound falsely reassuring (softening a genuine gap or a real recovery state to seem more pleasant).

**What tone is explicitly forbidden:** cheerful salesmanship of any kind; guilt or shame directed at a person's past purchases; gamification language (streaks, levels, badges — structurally impossible anyway, given no accounts exist to track them); corporate hedge-speak; performed empathy ("we know how frustrating that is!"); and excessive apology, especially around a genuine tie or a genuine data gap, both of which are honest outcomes, not failures the product owes an apology for.

## Part 2 — Conversation Patterns, By Experience

### Home
Intent capture reads as an open invitation, never a form with required fields. The one permitted Clarifying Question is phrased as a genuine question about what's meant, never as a menu of options dressed up as a question. The Out-of-Scope response restates all four real capabilities plainly and completely — never defensively, never apologetically, and never with a vague "we can't help with that" that omits what the product *can* do.

### Recommendation
Each question asks for exactly what's needed next, never a broader "tell us about yourself" framing that implies more is being collected than actually is. The canon's own structural pattern for confidence should govern every explanation here: state what's certain first, then shift language to mark what's a judgment call — *"I'm confident about the price and the value math; based on what you told me, this also seems to fit the style you're after"* is the literal, already-canonical example, and the shift from "I'm confident" to "seems to" is doing real communicative work, not just varying the sentence. A genuine tie is stated as plainly as the canon already requires for Comparison's tie language, since both share the same philosophy: *"these are equivalent on everything you've told me matters"* — never softened, never apologized for, never presented as an unsatisfying non-answer.

### Beer Detail
Facts are stated, not sold — no persuasive framing on a Legal Price, an ABV, or a Style Standing; they're presented as what they are. A Confirm-as-Is judgment, whenever it exists, is always paired with its Explanation in the same breath — never a bare verdict. "Legal Price" is never replaced with "correct price," even informally, since "correct" implies a moral judgment the canon deliberately avoids.

### Price Verification
The verdict is stated as a checkable fact, sourced explicitly to the government-published legal price, so the claim itself is falsifiable by the person reading it — never phrased as an opinion. **The tone here is the most tightly constrained in the entire product:** never celebratory when a good price is confirmed (no "great deal!" register — the appropriate feeling, per Product Definition's own framing, is quiet vindication, not triumph), and never editorializing about a retailer's intent when an overcharge is found — the product states the fact of the discrepancy, never a claim about why it happened or whether it was deliberate. This is a genuinely new, reasoned rule, not directly cited from any single canonical document, but a direct consequence of Price Verification's own Accepted architectural decision that it never escalates, extended here from behavior into language.

### Comparison *(specified, not yet built)*
A Trade-off is always framed against what the person actually said mattered, never against what the engine assumes they'd prefer — the canon's own worked contrast makes this precise: *"this is different from your stated style, but meaningfully better value, since you didn't set a hard style limit"* is correct; *"this is objectively the smarter pick"* is exactly the forbidden alternative. "Better" and "Recommended" are never used interchangeably — a Comparison winner is described as better *on stated grounds within the named set*, never as "the recommended beer." A tie is stated as plainly here as everywhere else it appears.

### Search/Browse *(no Screen Contract exists)*
Whatever language eventually governs this surface must contain no evaluative framing at all — no "best match," no "top pick," no implied ranking of any kind, since this surface matches, it doesn't judge. **[Recovered from Generation 1, compatible with this constraint]** Generation 1's zero-result copy pattern — "help us add this," an invitation to contribute rather than a dead end — is a genuine, portable tone lesson worth preserving *even though the underlying mechanism it originally served (crowdsourced submission) is permanently rejected*. The lesson that survives is the tone, not the feature: turn a real gap into an honest invitation rather than a wall, without ever implying a submission pipeline that doesn't exist.

## Part 3 — Explanation Templates (Structural Patterns, Not Copy)

These are the shapes an explanation should take — the actual words belong to a future copy pass, not this document.

**The Certainty-then-Judgment shift** (already canonical for Recommendation, extended here as a general pattern for any surface producing an Explanation): state the Verified/Computed facts first, in confident language; shift explicitly, in the same sentence or the next one, to softer language the moment a Soft Preference or judgment call enters — the shift itself is the signal, not a footnote explaining the shift.

**The Bounded-Comparison shape** (Comparison's Trade-off, generalized): name the specific dimension being weighed, name what the person actually stated about it, then state the trade-off plainly — never implying the comparison generalizes beyond the two things actually being compared.

**The Plain-Tie shape:** state that the options are equivalent, name specifically *what* they're equivalent on ("on everything you've told me matters," or the Comparison-specific equivalent), and stop — no manufactured tiebreaker, no apology, no "you can't go wrong either way" filler that implies the product is uncomfortable with its own honest answer.

**[Undefined — flagged, not resolved here]** no canonical explanation template exists yet for Price Verification's own verdict. The *structure* (three separated confidence dimensions: identification, legal reference, verification outcome) is specified; the *shape of the sentences* stating them is not, and should be drafted with the same certainty-then-judgment discipline as Recommendation's, once that work is undertaken.

## Part 4 — Recovery Communication Patterns

**State the fact, state why (if known), state what happens next — in that order, every time.** A recovery message that leads with an apology before the fact itself buries the one thing the person actually needs.

**Never over-apologize.** A genuine tie, a genuine data gap, or a genuine "nothing satisfies what you asked for" are honest outcomes the product is built to produce on purpose — they are not failures owed an apology, and treating them as such would quietly contradict the entire confidence-honesty principle by implying the honest answer was somehow a mistake.

**[Recovered from Generation 1]** the empty-state honesty pattern — showing what *is* known even when the one thing being asked for isn't ("No confirmed price yet" rather than hiding the item or showing a placeholder that could be mistaken for a real number) — is fully compatible with Gen 2's principles and should govern how any current or future missing-fact display reads. The specific Generation 1 wording ("be the first," tied to crowdsourcing) does not survive, since the mechanism it invited participation in is rejected — but the underlying discipline (show what's real, name what's missing, never fake completeness) does.

**Recovery language never assigns blame** — not to the person for asking something the product can't answer, and not to any external party (a retailer, a data source) for a gap the product itself hasn't resolved.

## Part 5 — Confidence Communication Rules

Never a number, a percentage, or a star rating, under any circumstance — this is the single most heavily and repeatedly enforced rule in the entire canon, restated here specifically as a language rule rather than a design rule.

Confidence is communicated through **which register a sentence is written in**, not through a separate confidence label bolted onto an otherwise neutral sentence — a Verified Fact reads as stated fact; a Human Judgment reads with visible hedging language ("seems to," "based on what you've told me") that a Verified Fact never carries.

A hedge is never used without saying, in the same breath, what it's hedging against — "probably" or "likely" with no stated reason is a form of vagueness the product's own honesty principle forbids just as much as false certainty does.

Two separate confidence layers (as in Comparison, per-candidate versus result-level) are never merged into one sentence that obscures which layer a given hedge belongs to.

## Part 6 — Forbidden Language

**Directly canonical, from the Lexicon and every Screen Specification's copy requirements:**
- "Score" or "Rating," anywhere, under any framing.
- Bare, unqualified "Best."
- "Suggest" in place of "Recommend," where Recommendation's actual reasoning applies.
- "Match" or "Fit" used as a noun standing in for an outcome.
- "Verify" used loosely for anything outside Price Verification's own scope.
- "Confirm" used without "-as-Is," when referring to that specific Feature.
- "Personalize" / "Personalization" used casually, implying a capability the product doesn't have.
- "Correct price," in place of "Legal Price."
- "Alternative" used as a generic, unqualified catch-all in place of "Comparison" or "Trade-off."
- Any evaluative language on Search/Browse — "best match," "top pick," or anything implying ranking by quality.

**Reasoned extensions, flagged as this document's own inference, not directly cited canon:**
- Stock apology phrases ("sorry," "oops," "we're sorry to say") attached to an honest tie or an honest data gap.
- Exclamation points or celebratory language anywhere near a Price Verification outcome, in either direction.
- Superlatives that imply catalog-wide authority beyond what was actually checked, even informally — "the best beer around" is exactly the kind of claim Comparison's own bounded-claim rule already forbids at the structural level; this extends the same discipline to word choice generally, not only to Comparison specifically.
- Emoji, slang, or informal register that would undercut the plain, factual voice established in Part 1.
- First-person hedges that imply the product is guessing rather than reasoning from stated facts — "I think" with no grounding, as opposed to "based on what you've told me," which names its own grounding.
- Gamified language of any kind (streaks, levels, badges, "you're on a roll") — structurally incompatible with the no-accounts decision regardless of tone, since nothing persists across sessions to gamify.

## Part 7 — Undefined Communication Gaps Still Requiring Product Decisions

1. **No canonical explanation template exists for Price Verification's verdict sentences** — only the three-dimension structure is specified, not example phrasing (Part 3).
2. **No canonical guidance exists for the tone of the imprecise-charged-price gap**, once that gap is resolved as a reasoning matter — the language for whatever recovery mechanism eventually gets built there is entirely undefined.
3. **Whether any warmth beyond plain factuality is ever appropriate is a genuinely open, undecided Product Decision.** This document's own recommendation (Part 1: minimal personality, calm and plain) is an inference from the product's existing restraint principles, not a settled prior decision — a future Product Decision could reasonably choose a warmer register without contradicting anything else in this document's factual content, only its own tone recommendation.
4. **No canonical guidance exists for the tone of Comparison's "Ask why" re-surfacing action** — the behavior (re-surface existing content, generate nothing new) is specified; whether re-surfacing it should read identically to its first appearance or acknowledge that it's being repeated is undefined.
5. **No canonical guidance exists for how a Recovery message should read when the underlying gap is itself unresolved architecture** (for example, Search/Browse's non-existence) rather than an ordinary runtime condition (zero search results) — these are different *kinds* of absence, and this document does not resolve whether they should sound different to the person encountering them.
