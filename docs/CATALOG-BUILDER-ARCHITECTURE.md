# ValueBrew — Catalog Builder Architecture

*Not another Catalog Specification. Catalog Specification 1.0 already defines WHAT belongs in the catalog. This document defines HOW a beer moves from being discovered in the real world to becoming a trustworthy SKU inside ValueBrew — the lifecycle, the pipeline, the sourcing, the publication gate, and the tooling that will eventually operate all of it. Built from direct inspection of the KSBCL Beer Pricing Pipeline Architecture (frozen, built, five stages real and tested), the actual repository state (`catalog/`, `pricing_data/`, real pipeline run outputs), Catalog Specification 1.0, Beer Knowledge Model 2.0, Domain Model 1.0, Beer Entity Specification 1.0, the Architecture Reconciliation Report, the Product Decisions Register, the KSBCL Stage 3–5 product discussions, and the enterprise catalog research corpus. Nothing here redesigns anything already decided. Where this document's own inspection surfaces a fact that contradicts an existing assumption, that fact is named explicitly, not smoothed over.*

## Part 1 — Overall Philosophy

**Authoritative data vs. curated data.** An authoritative source answers a question with legal or regulatory force behind it — KSBCL's declared MRP is authoritative for "what is the legal price," full stop, the same way the Beer Knowledge Model already treats Legal Price. A curated source answers a question a human decided, using judgment, from evidence that isn't itself a government filing — a founder deciding "this beer's Style is Witbier" after reading a brewery's own label is curation, not authority, even when it's correct. The distinction matters because authoritative facts are *never* second-guessed by curation (a KSBCL price is never "corrected" by a founder's opinion of what it should be), while curated facts are *expected* to be revised as better evidence arrives, without that revision implying the original curator was careless.

**Verified vs. Computed vs. Manually Curated — reused exactly from Beer Knowledge Model 2.0 and Catalog Specification 1.0, not reinvented here.** *Verified* means observed directly from an authoritative or directly-inspected source (KSBCL's declared price; a brewery's own published ABV, read directly off a label or official page). *Computed* means mechanically derived from Verified inputs with no judgment involved (`cost_per_ml_alcohol`, `value_score`, `Style Standing` — all already precomputed exactly this way in the real `Sku`/`Benchmark` schema). *Curated* means a human made a judgment call from evidence that doesn't rise to Verified on its own (a Style classification assigned by a founder reading a can, in the absence of any brewery's own style taxonomy; Food Pairing, per Catalog Specification 1.0's own framing). **No fact is ever silently promoted between these tiers.** A Curated Style assignment does not become Verified merely because it's been correct for six months; it becomes Verified only if an actual authoritative source (a brewery's own published style sheet) is later found and cited in its place.

**Immutable vs. mutable facts.** Two structurally different kinds of fact live in this system, and conflating them is exactly the failure mode the KSBCL pipeline's own architecture was built to prevent (its `ksbcl_item_code` vs. `canonical_product_id` distinction, and its append-only `beer_price_history.csv`, are both instances of this same discipline applied one layer down). **Immutable, once-true-always-true facts** — a price actually charged on a specific date, a classification decision made in a specific pipeline run, a KSBCL Item Code once assigned — are never edited or deleted; they are appended to a history and superseded, never overwritten. **Mutable, currently-true facts** — a SKU's current Legal Price, a Beer's current assigned Style, whether a canonical product is currently `LIVE` — are the *materialized, current* view computed from the immutable history, and are exactly what `catalog.json` publishes at any given moment. This is the same principle the pipeline's own `beer_master.csv` (mutable, overwritten each run) and `beer_price_history.csv` (immutable, append-only) already encode at the pricing layer; this document extends it, by direct analogy, to every other catalog field a future catalog-build step will need to manage the same way.

**Why manual enrichment exists — not a temporary limitation, a structural necessity.** Per §0.3 above, the KSBCL pipeline's real output contains no ABV, no Style, and no marketing-brand identity distinct from the licensed supplier of record. This is not a gap the pipeline failed to close — it is a direct, confirmed consequence of what the source PDF actually contains (§1 of the pipeline architecture document: "There is no category column. None."). No automated source for ABV exists anywhere in the researched landscape either (Architecture Reconciliation Report, confirmed independently across every brewery, retailer, and open-dataset source examined). **Manual enrichment therefore exists because the two things a launch-critical catalog needs most — ABV and Style — have no automated source at all, government or otherwise, for the large majority of real Karnataka SKUs.** This is why Part 4 below treats every enrichment field's "Who" and "When" as load-bearing questions, not administrative detail.

## Part 2 — Catalog Lifecycle

Eight stages, five of which (1–5) are frozen, built, and tested against real 2026-06 data; three of which (6–8, plus the recurring ninth) are specified here for the first time.

### Stage 1 — New SKU Discovered
**Inputs:** KSBCL's monthly "Supplier-wise Item-wise Price List" PDF, published by the Government of Karnataka.
**Outputs:** `structured_rows.csv` — every row in the source PDF, all categories, not yet filtered to beer (33,867 rows/month, confirmed).
**Owner:** the KSBCL pipeline, Stage 1 (`extract.py`), fully automated.
**Artifacts:** `pricing_data/runs/YYYY-MM/structured_rows.csv`, `pipeline.log`, `rejected_rows.csv`.
**Failure conditions:** a page yields zero recognizable rows; a column-header mismatch against the expected schema; rejected-row count exceeds threshold — any of these abort the run rather than publish incomplete output (pipeline architecture §8.2).

### Stage 2 — Pipeline Identity Resolution (Classification + Canonical Identity)
**Inputs:** `structured_rows.csv`.
**Outputs:** `classification_audit.csv` (every row, beer/not_beer/needs_review + confidence tier), then `normalized_rows.csv` (Stage 3's formatting/structural cleanup), then `item_code_canonical_map.csv` (Stage 4's stable `canonical_product_id` assignment, confirmed 1,715 rows live today).
**Owner:** the KSBCL pipeline, Stages 2–4 (`classify.py`, `normalize.py`, `resolve_canonical.py`), fully automated, human review only for flagged edge cases (`classification_review_queue.csv`, confirmed 2,897 pending rows; `canonical_resolution_review.csv`, confirmed 48 rows flagged in the 2026-06 run: 26 `ambiguous_key_multiple_candidates`, 22 `possible_supersession`).
**Artifacts:** `pricing_data/classification_known_terms.csv`, `classification_review_queue.csv`, `item_code_canonical_map.csv`, plus every run's dated audit trail under `pricing_data/runs/YYYY-MM/`.
**Failure conditions:** an extraction-bug-shaped duplicate (same Item Code twice in one run) hard-fails; a genuine ambiguous match writes to the review queue and blocks nothing else; **per §0.2 above, a confirmed live failure mode is a false-positive beer classification that this stage's own exclusion guard does not yet catch** — this is a real, present validation gap, not a theoretical one, and is carried forward into Part 7 rather than resolved here.

### Stage 3 — Canonical SKU Creation
**Inputs:** resolved, canonicalized rows from Stage 4/5 of the pipeline.
**Outputs:** `beer_master.csv` (1,005 live retail rows) and `beer_master_duty_free.csv` (152 live rows), one row per live canonical product, plus `beer_price_history.csv` (append-only, 1,715 rows and growing).
**Owner:** the KSBCL pipeline, Stage 5 (`history.py`), fully automated.
**Artifacts:** `pricing_data/beer_master.csv`, `beer_master_duty_free.csv`, `beer_price_history.csv`, `archive/beer_master_YYYY-MM.csv` (dated rollback snapshots).
**Failure conditions:** none beyond what Stages 1–2 already gate — Stage 5 itself is a deterministic diff against prior state, per the pipeline's own §7.

**This is where the frozen KSBCL pipeline's own scope ends.** Everything below is new architecture, specified by this document.

### Stage 4 — Manual Enrichment
**Inputs:** `beer_master.csv` rows that have cleared classification and identity resolution — i.e., a real, licensed, canonically-identified SKU that KSBCL confirms is legally sold in Karnataka, but that still lacks ABV, Style, brand/brewery marketing identity, and every other field the pipeline structurally cannot produce (§0.3).
**Outputs:** a set of Curated-confidence claims, each attributable to a specific enrichment source and a specific human or process, joined to the SKU by `canonical_product_id` — never merged directly into `beer_master.csv` itself, which remains the pipeline's own untouched output (see Part 4 for the field-by-field breakdown, and Part 8 for the tooling this stage will eventually run through).
**Owner:** a human — today, the founder; later, whoever the founder delegates enrichment review to. This is explicitly **not** automatable per Part 1's "why manual enrichment exists," and Part 10 restates this directly.
**Artifacts:** none exist yet in the repository — this is the first genuinely new artifact class this document calls for. See Part 8 for what tooling would need to produce and consume it.
**Failure conditions:** an enrichment claim sourced to nothing citable (see Part 7's "unsourced enrichment" rule); an enrichment claim that contradicts a Verified fact without flagging the contradiction (per Part 1's immutability discipline, a later-arriving authoritative source should never silently overwrite a Curated claim — both should coexist, with the authoritative one winning presentation, exactly as the Beer Knowledge Model already requires for price disagreements).

### Stage 5 — Validation
**Inputs:** a candidate SKU record, KSBCL-sourced fields plus whatever enrichment claims exist for it.
**Outputs:** either a pass (eligible for publication) or a rejection with a named, specific reason (never a silent drop).
**Owner:** automated rule-checking, human-reviewed only for borderline/flagged cases — see Part 7 for the full rule set.
**Artifacts:** a validation report, not yet built; conceptually the direct successor to the pipeline's own existing `rejected_rows.csv`/`run_summary.json` pattern, extended to cover fields the pipeline itself doesn't validate today (ABV plausibility, Style consistency, image licensing).
**Failure conditions:** by definition, this stage's entire purpose is to surface failure conditions — see Part 7.

### Stage 6 — Publication
**Inputs:** every SKU that passed Stage 5 validation.
**Outputs:** a decision, per SKU, of whether it enters the next `catalog.json` build — see Part 5 for the exact admission rule.
**Owner:** the catalog-build step itself (automated), with human override available for genuinely judgment-bound edge cases (e.g., a SKU that technically passes validation but a founder wants held back for a business reason — the Feature Inventory's own precedent for founder-level judgment calls outside automated rules).
**Artifacts:** none built yet; conceptually a manifest of admitted/rejected SKUs for a given build, with rejection reasons preserved for the next enrichment cycle to act on.
**Failure conditions:** a SKU missing a Launch-Critical field per the Catalog Specification's own classification is never published, no exception.

### Stage 7 — `catalog.json` Generation
**Inputs:** the admitted SKU set from Stage 6, joined against the Style/Benchmark/Beer entities the app's real `Catalog` domain model already expects (`shared_domain/beer.dart`, `shared_domain/sku.dart`, `catalog/domain/style.dart`, `catalog/domain/benchmark.dart` — all real, already-shipped Dart classes).
**Outputs:** a new `catalog.json`, matching the exact schema the app's `Beer.fromJson`/`Sku.fromJson`/`Benchmark.fromJson` factories already parse — this document does not propose changing that schema, only how it gets filled.
**Owner:** the catalog-build step (automated), producing a single versioned artifact (`catalog_version` already exists as a field in the real `catalog.json` and should simply increment, exactly as it's clearly designed to).
**Artifacts:** `catalog/catalog.json` itself, plus (new) a build manifest recording which pipeline run and which enrichment snapshot produced this specific catalog version — the direct analogue of `beer_master.csv`'s own `source_pdf_reference` field, one layer up.
**Failure conditions:** any SKU that would fail the app's own `fromJson` parsing (a null in a non-nullable Dart field) must never reach this output — this is the hard technical floor beneath Part 5's product-level publication rules.

### Stage 8 — App Consumption
**Inputs:** the published `catalog.json`.
**Outputs:** whatever the app already does with it — no change proposed here. Confirmed unchanged from the real, shipped `catalogProvider` and every screen this session's four Experience Specifications already documented in detail.
**Owner:** the Flutter app, exactly as it exists today.
**Artifacts:** none new.
**Failure conditions:** none new — the app's existing loading/error handling (`ErrorStateView`, `SkeletonBox`, already confirmed correct across Recommendation, Beer Detail, and Price Verification) is the only consumer-side failure surface, and it already works.

### Stage 9 (recurring) — Future Monthly Updates
Not a ninth stage so much as Stages 1–8 run again, every month, against a new KSBCL PDF — see Part 6 for exactly what regenerates, what's preserved, and what needs human attention each cycle.

**Government vs. Retailer vs. Manufacturer vs. Manual Observation vs. Future Enrichment, stated once, plainly, since the table above uses all five repeatedly:** Government is KSBCL, and only KSBCL, for anything price- or legal-supply-related — the one source with regulatory force. Retailer (Madhuloka, per the enterprise catalog research's own acquisition-strategy conclusion) is breadth-and-cross-check material, never authoritative for price or ABV. Manufacturer is a brewery's own published material (a label, an official product page) — the only source this document treats as strong enough to promote ABV or calories above Curated toward something closer to Verified, and even then, attributed to the manufacturer as a distinct verifying authority from KSBCL, never merged into KSBCL's own price-authoritative fields (pipeline architecture §10.3, reused here exactly as written). Manual Observation is a human — today the founder — directly reading a label, a can, or a brewery's own page and recording what it says, with the observation itself as the citable evidence. Future Enrichment is any source not yet adopted (Open Food Facts, GS1, a future retailer partnership) — named in Part 3's table only where the Architecture Reconciliation Report or the enterprise research corpus already confirmed it as a real, usable candidate, never invented fresh here.

## Part 4 — Enrichment Workflow

*For every field the request named, in the order given.*

**ABV**
*Why:* the single most consequential missing field — it's the direct input to `costPerMlAlcohol`, and therefore to Value Score, Style Standing, and Recommendation's entire ranking logic. Without it, a SKU cannot be meaningfully compared to any other. *When:* immediately after a SKU clears Stage 3 (Canonical SKU Creation) and before it can be considered for publication — this is the field Stage 5 Validation should gate hardest on, per Part 5. *Who:* the founder today; a delegated enrichment reviewer later. *Source:* Manufacturer (a brewery's own published label or official page), manually observed and cited — never estimated from a "typical style" figure (pipeline architecture §9, "Explicit data-gap flags... Don't backfill with assumed/typical-style ABV — leave... unset... rather than guessing," an enterprise-research finding this document adopts directly since it matches the Beer Knowledge Model's own never-invent principle exactly). *Confidence:* Curated (Manufacturer-sourced, manually cited) — never Verified, since KSBCL itself never states it. *Launch requirement:* per the Product Decisions Register's own D1, this is currently a blocking, undecided Product Decision (whether an incomplete-ABV candidate is excluded, included with a caveat, or handled some other way) — this document does not resolve it, only names where in the lifecycle it bites. *Future requirement:* the eventual target is 100% ABV coverage for every live SKU before it's fully trusted in Recommendation's ranking, though no document anywhere commits to a coverage threshold or timeline.

**Style**
*Why:* required for Style Benchmark, Style Standing, and Recommendation's style-refinement step. *When:* immediately after Canonical SKU Creation, same stage as ABV. *Who:* founder/delegated reviewer. *Source:* Manual curation — no automated source exists (Domain Model 1.0's own flagged inconsistency, §0 of the Product Decisions Register's D20: "the app's domain model treats Style as foundational and always-known... nothing in the actual KSBCL pipeline currently assigns it reliably at scale"). *Confidence:* Curated. *Launch requirement:* every launch SKU needs a Style assignment before it can compute a Benchmark-dependent field, though the SKU can still publish with Style Standing gracefully omitted (Beer Detail Screen Contract's own explicit rule) if Style itself is confidently known but the *Benchmark* population is too thin. *Future requirement:* same open question as D20 in the Product Decisions Register — what happens to a real catalog Beer that genuinely has no confident Style assignment yet — carried forward here, not resolved.

**Brewery**
*Why:* Beer Identity's own Brewery field, and the basis for the Architecture Reconciliation Report's own confirmed contract-brewing distinction (Budweiser bottled by S P R Distilleries; Guinness imported by Brindco). *When:* at the same enrichment pass as Style/ABV. *Who:* founder/delegated reviewer. *Source:* the KSBCL pipeline's own `supplier_name`/`supplier_code` fields give the *licensed supplier*, which is Government-sourced and Verified — but per the Architecture Reconciliation Report's own Category C2 finding, this is not always the same thing as the marketing-facing "brewed by" identity a person would recognize. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** the app's real `Beer.brewery` field is currently populated as a single string with no distinction between "licensed supplier" and "brand owner" — the Architecture Reconciliation Report already named this as a Product Decision ("would change how 'Brewery' should ever be displayed... the Conversation Model currently has no rule for this distinction at all") and this document does not resolve it either, only confirms it's still live. *Confidence:* the licensed-supplier string is Verified (KSBCL-sourced); any marketing-brand distinction layered on top would be Curated. *Launch requirement:* the licensed-supplier string alone is sufficient for launch. *Future requirement:* resolving the brand-owner-vs-supplier distinction, per the still-open Architecture Reconciliation Report finding.

**Images**
*Why:* per the Catalog Specification's own singling-out of this field, "plausibly needed for basic usability regardless of any deeper enrichment ambition." *When:* Product Enrichment tier, per Catalog Specification 1.0 — not gating Publication unless a future Product Decision says otherwise. *Who:* founder/delegated reviewer, or a licensed external source if adopted. *Source:* Manual curation (photographed) or a licensed retailer/open-dataset source with confirmed, checkable licensing (Open Food Facts, CC BY-SA) — never hotlinked or reused without a checkable license, a discipline this document adopts directly from the enterprise research corpus's own `image.license`/`license_requires_attribution` design. *Confidence:* Curated. *Launch requirement:* explicitly undecided (Product Decisions Register D15) — this document flags it as an open small Product Decision, per that register, and takes no position. *Future requirement:* if pursued, a real license-tracking discipline per SKU/image, not just a bare URL.

**Flavor, Aroma, Bitterness, Body, Sweetness, Ingredients** (grouped, since Catalog Specification 1.0 already treats this cluster identically)
*Why:* named only because they appeared as illustrative examples in a prior request; the Catalog Specification is explicit that "there is no source of truth, no confidence rule, and no existing Product Decision behind any of them." *When:* not scheduled — Future Expansion, deliberately unscoped. *Who:* undetermined. *Source:* none exists. *Confidence:* would be Curated at best, and the Catalog Specification itself warns any of these "would need careful review against the Lexicon before... sounding like a disguised rating." *Launch requirement:* none — explicitly excluded. *Future requirement:* none committed; folded into the Product Decisions Register's own D16 cluster.

**Food Pairing**
*Why:* the one item in the sensory cluster with real Generation 1 precedent — Generation 1's own architecture document named it as a designed Extension Point ("once pairing data exists in the catalog schema, this is a new `RecommendationReason` value plus one new `SimilarityStrategy` implementation") — a genuine, compatible, recoverable idea, not invented fresh here. *When:* not scheduled. *Who:* undetermined. *Source:* would require curated content. *Confidence:* Curated. *Launch requirement:* none. *Future requirement:* per Product Decisions Register D16, "still requiring a real Product Decision to prioritize" — this document does not make that decision.

**GTIN**
*Why:* barcode-scan capability, a reserved-but-empty seam already present in `beer_master.csv`'s own schema. *When:* not scheduled for launch; the pipeline architecture itself calls this "a reserved, empty seam, not a built feature" (§10.2). *Who:* undetermined; a future enrichment pass, if built, joining on `canonical_product_id` per the pipeline's own explicit design intent. *Source:* Open Food Facts is the only currently-safe, redistributable candidate with confirmed real coverage; Go-UPC is explicitly excluded on licensing grounds. *Confidence:* would be Curated/crowd-sourced-adjacent, always visibly labeled as such per the pipeline architecture's own §10.3 discipline. *Launch requirement:* none. *Future requirement:* an actual coverage audit before any barcode milestone is scheduled — flagged as still-open in the pipeline architecture's own §12.5.

**Awards, Brewery Story**
*Why/When/Who/Source/Confidence/Launch/Future:* identical treatment to the Flavor/Aroma cluster above — named, not specified, no existing source, Future Expansion, folded into Product Decisions Register D16.

## Part 5 — Publication Rules

**When may a SKU enter `catalog.json`?** Only once it has cleared every stage in Part 2 through Stage 5 (Validation) with no unresolved rejection, and carries every field the Catalog Specification 1.0 already classifies as **Launch Critical**, populated at least at Curated confidence. This document does not redefine that classification — it is reused exactly as Catalog Specification 1.0 Part 1 already states it.

**When must it be rejected?** Any of: a missing Launch Critical field (see below); a classification confidence the pipeline itself flags as `low` or `needs_review` and that hasn't cleared human review (`classification_review_queue.csv`'s own `review_status` field, reused directly — a `pending` row is never published); a failed validation rule from Part 7; an unresolved `canonical_resolution_review.csv` flag (a SKU still awaiting a possible-supersession decision is not yet a stable identity to publish under).

**Mandatory fields (block Publication entirely):** canonical identity (`canonical_product_id`), Beer Identity (name, brewery-as-licensed-supplier), Legal Price and its provenance (`priceSource`, `priceLastChecked`), size and package type, and — per the still-open Product Decisions Register item D1 — ABV, **pending that decision's resolution**. This document flags ABV as provisionally mandatory (the safer default, consistent with the Recommendation Experience Specification's own treatment of the null-ABV gap as "a blocking dependency for shipping... against real data") without pretending D1 is actually closed.

**Fields that block Recommendation specifically, without blocking Detail:** Style, and by extension Style Benchmark/Style Standing — per the Beer Detail Screen Contract's own explicit rule, a SKU with no Style Benchmark available still displays fully on Beer Detail, gracefully omitting only the Style Standing line; but Recommendation's own style-refinement step (per Decision Engine 2.0) cannot meaningfully rank a SKU against a style it doesn't have.

**Fields that only affect Detail, never block Publication or Recommendation:** GTIN, Images, and every Future Expansion field named in Part 4 — none of these are read by `generateRecommendation()` (confirmed directly against the real function's own logic in the Recommendation Widget Specification) or by any of the app's real screens' core rendering paths.

**Optional, genuinely no-consequence fields:** `isCraft` (Beer Entity Specification's own flagged low-stakes item, D14 in the Product Decisions Register — "nothing currently depends on it").

## Part 6 — Monthly Update Process

**What regenerates, every KSBCL update, exactly per the pipeline's own already-frozen Stage 1–5 behavior (not redesigned here):** `structured_rows.csv` is rebuilt from scratch each run (Stage 1 is stateless per-run). `classification_audit.csv` and `normalized_rows.csv` likewise regenerate fully each run. `item_code_canonical_map.csv` is **append-mostly** — existing mappings are never deleted or renumbered, only added to or, on an explicit human-confirmed merge, repointed (pipeline architecture §4.4). `beer_master.csv`/`beer_master_duty_free.csv` are **overwritten** each run to reflect current live state — "each monthly PDF is KSBCL's assertion of current truth" (§7.2). `beer_price_history.csv` only **grows**, appending a new row exactly where the price tuple actually changed, never wholesale.

**What must additionally happen at the catalog-build layer, newly specified here:** the catalog-build step must re-run Stage 4 (Manual Enrichment) validity checks against the new `beer_master.csv` diff — specifically: which SKUs are newly `LIVE` (need fresh enrichment before they can publish), which existing SKUs changed identity-relevant fields (name, pack size — potentially invalidating a prior enrichment claim's applicability), and which SKUs went `DELISTED` (whose enrichment claims should be retained, not deleted, per Part 1's immutability principle, but excluded from the next `catalog.json`).

**How manual enrichments are protected.** Because enrichment claims are joined by `canonical_product_id` (Part 2, Stage 4) and `canonical_product_id` is itself append-mostly and stable across runs (the entire point of Stage 4 of the KSBCL pipeline, per its own §12.3 decision), an enrichment claim survives a monthly re-run automatically, as long as the SKU's `canonical_product_id` itself doesn't change. **The one real risk, worth naming explicitly:** a manual-review-confirmed merge (two canonical products found to be the same real product, per `canonical_resolution_review.csv`'s `possible_supersession` flow) repoints a `canonical_product_id` — any enrichment claim attached to the now-superseded ID would be orphaned unless the catalog-build step explicitly follows that repointing. **This is a genuine, newly-identified integration seam this document surfaces** — the KSBCL pipeline's own merge mechanism was designed without an enrichment layer in mind (it predates this document), and nothing in the pipeline's own architecture currently notifies a downstream enrichment store when a merge happens. This is not a Product Decision so much as an Engineering integration requirement for whoever eventually builds Stage 4 tooling (Part 8).

**How conflicts are surfaced.** Per Part 1's immutability discipline: never resolved by silent overwrite. A later Manufacturer-sourced ABV claim that disagrees with an earlier one does not erase the earlier one — both persist, with the more recent (or more authoritative, if the two differ in source type) one presented, exactly mirroring the Beer Knowledge Model's own existing rule for price disagreements ("that divergence should be surfaced as an additional, clearly labeled signal — never allowed to quietly become the new reference truth").

**What requires human review, every cycle:** every row in `classification_review_queue.csv` and `canonical_resolution_review.csv` that the pipeline itself already flags (unchanged, frozen behavior); every newly-`LIVE` SKU with no existing enrichment claim (a new candidate for Stage 4); any SKU whose Style/ABV enrichment claim's source SKU identity changed enough that the claim's continued applicability is genuinely in doubt (a judgment call, not a mechanical rule — deliberately so, since inventing a mechanical rule for "does this old claim still apply" would itself be guessing at a fact, exactly the thing Part 1 forbids).

## Part 7 — Validation

*Every rule below is either already enforced by the frozen pipeline (cited as such) or newly specified for the catalog-build step this document defines. The whiskey-contamination example from §0.2 is used directly, not hypothetically, wherever it's the clearest illustration.*

**Duplicate identities.** Already enforced by the pipeline: `canonical_product_id` unique within `beer_master.csv` by construction (Stage 4/5); `ksbcl_item_code` unique within `item_code_canonical_map.csv`, hard-failing the run otherwise (pipeline architecture §9). No new rule needed at the catalog-build layer.

**Invalid ABV.** Newly specified — the pipeline itself has no ABV field to validate. A catalog-build-layer rule: ABV must be a plausible percentage (a sanity range, e.g., not negative, not implausibly high for a beer — mirroring the pipeline's own `MRP > 0` / plausible-range discipline for price, §9); an ABV claim with no cited source is rejected outright, never silently accepted (per Part 4's own "never estimated" rule for this field).

**Missing mandatory fields.** Per Part 5's own mandatory-field list, checked at Stage 5.

**Style mismatch.** **This is where §0.2's whiskey-contamination finding belongs, directly, not as a hypothetical.** A newly specified rule: any SKU whose `item_name_raw` contains a spirit-family term from the pipeline's own existing exclusion-guard vocabulary (`whisky`/`whiskey`, `rum`, `brandy`, `vodka`, `gin`, per pipeline architecture §6.1) must be rejected at the catalog-build layer **regardless of its upstream `classification_confidence`**, closing exactly the gap that let both `CP0000001` and `CP0000955` through today. This is a deliberate, explicit second gate at the catalog-build layer, not a request to modify the frozen Stage 2 classifier itself — the pipeline's own exclusion guard is documented as vetoing "a *brand-only* match" but explicitly "never veto[ing] an unambiguous *style-keyword* match" (§6.1), which is precisely how the Glenfiddich "IPA Experiment" row slipped through at `high` confidence. Closing this at the catalog-build layer, as a second, independent check, is consistent with Part 1's own discipline that Verified/Computed facts are never blindly trusted forward without their own gate.

**Orphan references.** A Beer with no resolvable Style; a Sku with no resolvable Beer — mechanically checkable via the same `resolveBeer`/`resolveStyle` lookup pattern the real app's `catalog_lookups.dart` already implements, applied at build time instead of at read time so a broken reference never reaches `catalog.json` at all.

**Broken images.** A newly specified rule, per Part 4's own image-sourcing discipline: any image reference without a checkable, non-"unknown" license is rejected from publication, not merely flagged — reusing the enterprise research corpus's own explicit default ("default new rows to 'unknown-do-not-redistribute' until legal clears") as the safe posture for this document's own rule, since no different posture has ever been adopted by any frozen canonical document.

**Stale prices.** Already partially enforced by the pipeline (a `DELISTED` SKU is excluded from `beer_master.csv`'s live set automatically). A newly specified catalog-build-layer addition: `priceLastChecked` older than the source PDF's own `run_month` by more than one cycle should be flagged for review before publication, consistent with the still-open freshness question named in the Product Decisions Register (D11) — this document does not resolve whether a stronger staleness *display* signal should exist (that's D11's own scope), only that stale data shouldn't silently re-publish unreviewed.

**Listing inconsistencies.** Directly relevant to the still-open `Listing` entity question (Product Decisions Register D12): today, a single `representative_item_code` stands in for a canonical product's price (pipeline architecture §4.5) even when — per the real, confirmed 2026-06 data — a single SKU can have multiple concurrent supplier listings at materially different prices. **A validation rule this document does specify, independent of whether `Listing` is ever built as a real entity:** if a canonical product's mapped item codes disagree on price by more than a documented threshold, this should be surfaced for review before publication, not silently resolved by the pipeline's own existing "most recent effective_date wins" tie-break (§4.5) alone — the tie-break is correct for choosing *what to display*, but a large price spread across suppliers is itself a signal worth a human's attention, exactly the kind of thing Part 1's evidence-not-noise discipline calls for.

**Ranked, per the pipeline's own existing precedent for how badly silent failure would corrupt the catalogue (pipeline architecture §9, extended here one layer up to the catalog-build step specifically):**
1. Style mismatch / cross-category contamination — now *proven*, not theoretical, per §0.2.
2. Missing or unsourced ABV entering `catalog.json` — corrupts Recommendation's entire ranking silently, with no error signal, exactly the failure mode Product Decisions Register D1 already worries about.
3. An enrichment claim orphaned by a canonical-identity merge (Part 6) — silently loses real, curated work with no visible failure.
4. Broken/unlicensed image references — a legal and trust risk, not a ranking risk, but real.
5. Stale, unreviewed prices republishing without a human glance — lower severity, since KSBCL's own monthly cadence already bounds how stale "stale" can get.

## Part 8 — Tooling Roadmap

*Responsibilities only, per the explicit instruction — no UI design. The enterprise catalog research corpus's own "Production Database Schema" document (`docs/research/enterprise_catalog_research/03-production-database-schema.md`) sketches a much larger, national-scale, multi-table Postgres system with append-only claim ledgers, confidence scoring, and a nightly resolver — genuinely useful as a long-horizon reference for what these tools could eventually become, but **explicitly not adopted architecture**: it was produced as research, never promoted through a Product Decision (this is exactly what Product Decisions Register D19 — "no committed decision on the Beer Knowledge Base's real backend/data source" — is still waiting on), and this document does not treat any part of it as decided. The five tools below are scoped to what this document's own lifecycle (Part 2) actually requires, not to that larger research vision.*

**Catalog Builder.** Owns Stage 6–7 of Part 2: reading the current `beer_master.csv`/`beer_master_duty_free.csv` plus every current enrichment claim, applying Part 5's publication rules and Part 7's validation rules, and producing a new `catalog.json` plus its build manifest. The one piece of automation in this whole roadmap with zero human-in-the-loop requirement once its inputs are trustworthy — its entire job is mechanical application of already-decided rules.

**Enrichment Queue.** Owns surfacing which SKUs need Stage 4 (Manual Enrichment) attention: newly-`LIVE` SKUs with no existing claim, SKUs whose upstream identity changed enough to put an existing claim in doubt (Part 6), and SKUs specifically missing a Launch Critical field. Its responsibility ends at *surfacing* — it does not itself judge Style or ABV; it hands a human exactly the SKU and the evidence (KSBCL's `item_name_raw`, whatever prior claim exists) needed to make that judgment quickly.

**Validation Dashboard.** Owns presenting Part 7's rule outcomes in aggregate — how many SKUs fail each rule, this cycle vs. last cycle (directly mirroring the pipeline's own existing `run_summary.json` pattern, extended one layer up) — so a human can spot a systemic problem (a sudden spike in Style-mismatch rejections, say) rather than reviewing rejections one at a time with no sense of trend.

**Merge Review.** Owns presenting `canonical_resolution_review.csv`'s existing `possible_supersession`/`ambiguous_key_multiple_candidates` flags for human decision, and — per Part 6's own newly-identified integration seam — propagating a confirmed merge's `canonical_product_id` repointing into the enrichment-claim store so no Curated work is silently orphaned.

**Publication Preview.** Owns letting a human see what the *next* `catalog.json` would actually contain — which SKUs are newly added, newly removed, and what changed on existing ones — before that build is actually promoted to the app-facing artifact, mirroring the pipeline's own existing "candidate before accepted" discipline (§9's own framing: "before `beer_master.csv`/`beer_price_history.csv` are promoted from candidate to accepted") extended one layer up to the catalog itself.

## Part 10 — Executive Summary

**What is the minimum launch catalog?** Every SKU that clears Part 2's Stage 1–5 (frozen pipeline identity resolution, unchanged) plus Stage 4 (Manual Enrichment) for exactly the fields Part 5 marks Mandatory: canonical identity, Beer Identity, Legal Price with provenance, size/package, and ABV (provisional, per D1). Style is required to unlock Recommendation-quality ranking but does not, by itself, block a SKU from appearing on Beer Detail. Everything else named in Part 4 — Images, GTIN, the entire sensory/narrative cluster — is explicitly not required and does not block Publication.

**What makes a catalog trustworthy?** Two things, directly per Part 1's own philosophy: **provenance that's never lost** (every fact traceable to whether it's Government-Verified, Manufacturer-Curated, or Computed, exactly the discipline the KSBCL pipeline already enforces for pricing and this document extends to everything else) and **validation that actually catches what's already gone wrong** — Part 7's Style-mismatch rule exists specifically because §0.2 proved, with two real rows sitting in the current pipeline output today, that the frozen classifier alone isn't sufficient. Trustworthiness is not a property of any one source; it's the property of a system that never lets an unattributed or unvalidated fact reach the app silently.

**What can safely wait?** Everything Part 4 classifies as Product Enrichment or Future Expansion (Images pending its own small decision, Food Pairing, GTIN, the sensory cluster) — none of these are read by any real reasoning surface in the shipped app today, confirmed directly against the actual `generateRecommendation`, Beer Detail, and Price Verification source across this session's four Experience Specifications. The `Listing` entity (D12) can also safely wait — Part 7's validation rule for multi-supplier price spread is designed to function as a review flag without it existing yet.

**What should never be automated?** Style and ABV assignment, for as long as no automated source exists for either (§0.3's own confirmed finding) — Part 1's entire "why manual enrichment exists" argument rests on this, and no tool in Part 8 is scoped to guess either field; every tool's job is to surface the need for human judgment faster, never to replace it. Licensing decisions for any third-party image or data source (Part 7's broken-images rule) should also never be automated past a "reject if unlicensed" floor — determining whether a *new* source's license is actually usable is a judgment call, not a mechanical check.

**What should eventually become automated?** Everything Part 2's Stages 1–5 already automate, unchanged and not revisited here. Beyond that: Stage 6–7 (Publication and `catalog.json` generation, the Catalog Builder tool) is fully mechanical once its inputs are trustworthy and should be automated first, since it introduces no new judgment beyond applying already-decided rules. Style/ABV enrichment itself should stay manual indefinitely under this document's own reasoning — but *sourcing candidates* for a human to review (a future partnership feed, a barcode-matched enrichment source, per Part 3's Future Enrichment column) is exactly the kind of thing that could eventually reduce how much manual observation each new SKU requires, without ever removing the human judgment step entirely, consistent with D19's own still-undecided scope for how far that automation should ever go.
